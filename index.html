Peach vs. Nectarine

Same species, different skin. What really sets them apart?

VS
01

Where Do They Come From?

Both fruits are the same species: Prunus persica. Botanists classify the nectarine as a variety of the peach.

  1. China

    Peaches first grew in northwest China, where people have cultivated them for more than 4,000 years.

  2. Persia

    Traders carried them along the Silk Road to Persia. That is why the Latin name is persica, meaning “from Persia.”

  3. Europe

    The Greeks and Romans planted peach trees across Europe.

  4. Americas

    Spanish explorers brought peaches to the Americas in the 1500s.

Myth buster

Nectarines are not a cross between a peach and a plum. They appeared naturally on peach trees through a small genetic change, and a peach tree can even produce a nectarine now and then.

02

Peach vs. Nectarine

Same species, one small gene of difference.

Attribute-by-attribute comparison of peaches and nectarines.
Attribute Peach Nectarine
Skin Covered in tiny, soft hairs (fuzz) Smooth and shiny, with no fuzz
Genetics Same species; carries the dominant gene for fuzz Same species; two copies of a recessive gene remove the fuzz
Pit (stone) Hard stone in the center (clingstone or freestone types) Hard stone in the center (clingstone or freestone types)
Flesh Juicy and soft, with a mild sweetness Slightly firmer and often a little sweeter; no peeling needed
Size Usually a little bigger Usually a little smaller

Both are stone fruits (drupes) and belong to the rose family (Rosaceae), like cherries, plums and almonds.

04

In the Kitchen

Fresh

  • Eat them whole, juicy and ready to go
  • Slice them into salads, yogurt or oatmeal

Baked

  • Classic peach pie, cobbler and crumble
  • Grilled or roasted for a warm, caramelized dessert

Preserved

  • Jam, syrup and canned fruit that last all year
  • Dried slices for a chewy, sweet snack

Drinks

  • Smoothies, juices and iced tea
  • The Bellini: peach purée with sparkling wine

Tip: nectarines hold their shape well on the grill, and their smooth skin is thin and edible, so there is no need to peel them.

Fun Facts

Rose family?

Peaches and nectarines belong to the rose family, along with apples, cherries, plums and almonds.

Fruit of immortality

Tap to flip

In Chinese legend, eating a peach gives long life. Peach trees symbolize luck and longevity, and “longevity peach” buns are still served at birthdays.

Bellini

Tap to flip

The Bellini was created in Venice around 1948. It is made with white peach purée and sparkling wine, and it is named after the painter Giovanni Bellini.
